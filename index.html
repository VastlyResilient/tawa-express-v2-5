Why The Tactics Work — The Research Behind Each One

Marketing that works on how brains actually decide.

Mere-Exposure Effect
Zajonc, “Attitudinal Effects of Mere Exposure,” 1968
→ Applied
Repeated contact breeds preference. Near-daily posting for 30 days makes Tawa the familiar, default option before taste ever gets a vote.
Social Proof
Cialdini, Influence (1984/2021)
→ Applied
People follow visible people. Reposted customer tables, reviews and “Friday night” shots let diners see others choosing Tawa — the core driver of restaurant choice.
We Eat With Our Eyes
Spence et al., Flavour (2016); picture-superiority, Nelson et al. (1976)
→ Applied
Visual presentation measurably shifts perceived taste and value. Images are remembered far better than text — so the dish photo carries the message, not the caption.
Reciprocity
Cialdini, Influence (1984/2021)
→ Applied
Reposting a customer is a small public gift. The norm of reciprocity makes them return, re-tag and bring friends — a self-fueling content engine at $0.
Processing Fluency
Reber & Schwarz (1999) — easy-to-process statements feel truer
→ Applied
Plain sentences (“Indian fusion, 5 minutes from UB”) beat clever ones. Fluency reads as credible — and it's exactly what Instagram & AI search index best.
Customer Is the Hero
Miller, Building a StoryBrand (2017)
→ Applied
Brands that position themselves as the guide — not the hero — convert better. The bio rewrite sells their Tuesday-night dinner, not “our journey.”
